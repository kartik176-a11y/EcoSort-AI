# Wet/Biodegradable Waste Guidance

[Overview]
Wet or biodegradable waste includes food scraps, fruit and vegetable peels, leftover food, garden waste, and other organic materials that decompose naturally.

[Household Examples]
- Fruit peels (banana, orange, apple, etc.)
- Vegetable scraps and peels
- Cooked food leftovers
- Eggshells
- Tea leaves and coffee grounds
- Flower and garden waste
- Small amounts of paper towels soiled with food

[Proper Disposal]
Keep wet waste separate from dry recyclables in a dedicated wet-waste bin. This prevents contamination of recyclable materials.

[Composting]
Wet organic waste is ideal for composting. Home composting can reduce waste volume and create nutrient-rich soil amendment for gardens.

[Municipal Collection]
Many cities including Vizag have separate collection for wet waste. Check your local schedule for wet-waste collection days.

[What to Avoid]
Do not mix wet waste with dry recyclables, hazardous materials, or general trash. Do not include:
- Plastic bags or wrappers
- Bones (in some programs)
- Large woody materials
- Diseased plants

[Environmental Benefits]
Proper segregation of wet waste:
- Reduces landfill methane emissions
- Enables composting and biogas production
- Prevents contamination of recyclables
- Supports circular economy

[Vizag Context]
Greater Visakhapatnam Municipal Corporation (GVMC) encourages household-level wet-waste segregation. Residents should maintain separate bins for wet and dry waste.
