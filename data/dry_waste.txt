# Dry/Recyclable Waste Guidance

[Overview]
Dry recyclable waste includes materials that can be processed and reused to create new products. This includes paper, cardboard, plastic, glass, and metal items.

[Common Recyclables]

**Paper and Cardboard:**
- Newspapers and magazines
- Office paper and notebooks
- Cardboard boxes (flatten before recycling)
- Paper bags
- Cartons (milk, juice boxes)

**Plastic:**
- Plastic bottles (water, soda, juice)
- Plastic containers and jars
- Plastic bags (clean and dry)
- Note: Not all plastic types are recyclable everywhere

**Glass:**
- Glass bottles and jars
- Clean glass containers
- Note: Remove lids and caps

**Metal:**
- Aluminum cans (soda, beer)
- Tin cans (food containers)
- Metal containers
- Aluminum foil (clean)

[Preparation Guidelines]
1. **Empty and clean** containers where practical
2. **Remove labels** if required by local program
3. **Flatten** cardboard boxes to save space
4. **Keep dry** - wet materials may contaminate batch
5. **Separate by type** if required by your local program

[What NOT to Include]
- Food-soiled paper or cardboard
- Ceramics or heat-resistant glass
- Plastic bags (check local rules)
- Hazardous containers
- Electronic waste
- Batteries

[Storage at Home]
Maintain a separate dry-waste bin. Keep it clean and dry. Store recyclables until collection day or take to collection center.

[Environmental Impact]
Recycling dry waste:
- Conserves natural resources
- Reduces energy consumption vs. virgin materials
- Decreases landfill burden
- Reduces greenhouse gas emissions
- Supports green jobs and circular economy

[Vizag/GVMC Context]
GVMC promotes source segregation of waste. Dry recyclables should be stored separately from wet waste. Regular collection schedules vary by area - check with your local sanitation worker or GVMC office.

[Quality Matters]
Contaminated recyclables may be rejected. Keep food waste, liquids, and hazardous materials out of the dry-waste stream.
